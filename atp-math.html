CURRICULUM OVERVIEW

The IFB AE-S Mathematics curriculum is not a standard high school syllabus. It is a rigorous, university-level bridging course designed to force students to master abstract, logical, and computational thinking without relying on rote memorization or formula sheets.


Students are expected to complete approximately 8 to 10 hours of self-study per week outside of lectures. The curriculum is strictly divided into advanced pillars:

  • Advanced Algebra: Matrices, Complex Numbers, Partial Fractions, and rigid inequalities.
  • Functions & Geometry: Deep analysis of parabolic, cubic, exponential, and logarithmic graphs, plus 3D analytical geometry.
  • Calculus: Limits, first principles, advanced differentiation, integration, and real-world optimization.
  • Probability & Data: Bayes' Theorem, 3-set Venn diagrams, and advanced statistics.

Assessment Structure: The course features four rigorous examination papers (Paper 1, 2, 3, and 4) totaling 600 Marks. No formula sheets are provided; students must derive or memorize all necessary mathematical identities.

TERM 1: ADVANCED ALGEBRA & FUNCTIONS
Week Topic Content & Sub-topics
1 - 2 Polynomials & Partial Fractions Remainder and Factor Theorem. Factorising cubics. Decomposing complex rational expressions into partial fractions.
3 - 4 Matrices & Determinants Matrix addition, multiplication, and scalar multiplication. Finding the inverse of a 2x2 and 3x3 matrix using determinants. Solving simultaneous equations using Cramer's Rule.
5 - 6 Complex Numbers Introduction to imaginary numbers (\(i\)). Operations with complex numbers. Argand diagrams. Polar form and De Moivre's Theorem.
7 - 8 Advanced Inequalities Solving rational inequalities \(\left(\frac{f(x)}{g(x)} \ge 0\right)\) using critical values and sign tables. Absolute value equations and inequalities.
9 - 10 Functions & Inverses Domain, range, and asymptotes. Transformations of graphs. Finding the inverse of linear, quadratic, and exponential functions.
11 Term 1 Assessment AE-S Paper 1: Advanced Algebra, Matrices & Functions (150 Marks).

TERM 2: CALCULUS
Week Topic Content & Sub-topics
1 - 2 Limits & First Principles Evaluating limits. Using \(f'(x) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h}\) for quadratics, cubics, and rational functions.
3 - 4 Advanced Differentiation Product rule, quotient rule, and chain rule. Differentiating trigonometric, exponential, and logarithmic functions.
5 - 6 Applications of Derivatives Equations of tangents and normals. Using \(f'(x)=0\) and \(f''(x)\) to find and classify stationary points. Curve sketching.
7 - 8 Introduction to Integration Anti-differentiation (indefinite integrals). The fundamental theorem of calculus. Definite integrals.
9 - 10 Area & Volume Calculating the area under a curve between two points. Calculating the volume of a solid of revolution.
11 Term 2 Assessment AE-S Paper 2: Differential and Integral Calculus (150 Marks).
