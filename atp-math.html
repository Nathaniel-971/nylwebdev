ACADEMIC ROADMAP

The IFB-S Mathematics curriculum is an uncompromising, university-level bridge designed for the 2027 academic year. It operates at maximum capacity, eliminating rote learning to enforce rigorous abstract logic, proof, and advanced computation.


Workload: Students must commit to a minimum of 10 to 12 hours of independent study per week outside of lectures.


Assessment: No formula sheets are provided during any assessment; all identities must be derived or memorized. The course features four rigorous examination papers totaling 600 Marks.


Curriculum Structure: The year is divided into 9 distinct mathematical pillars, each allocated a specific number of weeks to ensure comprehensive mastery before moving to the next logical progression.

ELABORATION OF CONTENT: ALGEBRA
WeekTopicElaboration of ContentAssess
1 Algebra
  • Exponents: Mastery of the laws of exponents for rational, negative, and zero exponents. Applying these laws to simplify complex algebraic fractions.
  • Surds: Simplifying surds, multiplying and dividing surds, and rationalising the denominator (both monomial and binomial denominators using conjugates).
2 Algebra
  • Exponential Equations: Solving equations where bases can be equated. Solving equations requiring quadratic substitution (e.g., \(2^{2x} - 5 \cdot 2^x + 4 = 0\)).
  • Quadratic Equations: Solving using factorisation, completing the square, and the quadratic formula.
  • Nature of Roots: Deep analysis using the discriminant (\(\Delta = b^2 - 4ac\)). Determining conditions for real, non-real, equal, and distinct roots. Finding unknown parameters (e.g., \(k\)) to satisfy specific root conditions.
3 Algebra
  • Cubic & Quartic Polynomials: Applying the Remainder and Factor Theorems to polynomials of degree 3 and 4.
  • Factorisation: Factorising expressions of the form \(ax^3+bx^2+cx+d\) and \(ax^4+\dots\)
  • Synthetic Division: Using synthetic division to efficiently find roots and reduce polynomial degrees.
4 Algebra
  • Simultaneous Equations: Solving systems involving one linear and one quadratic equation (algebraically and graphically).
  • 3x3 Systems: Solving three linear equations with three unknowns using substitution, elimination, or matrices.
  • Word Problems: Translating complex real-world problems into simultaneous equations and solving them.
5 Algebra
  • Inequalities: Solving quadratic inequalities using the parabolic method and critical values.
  • Rational Inequalities: Solving inequalities like \(\frac{x-1}{x+3} \ge \frac{x}{x-2}\) using sign tables and interval notation.
  • Absolute Values: Solving absolute value equations and inequalities (linear and quadratic).
6 Algebra
  • Matrices: Performing matrix operations (addition, subtraction, scalar multiplication, matrix multiplication). Calculating determinants and finding the inverse of 2x2 and 3x3 matrices.
  • Cramer's Rule: Solving 2x2 and 3x3 systems using inverse matrices.
  • Complex Numbers: Introduction to \(i^2 = -1\), performing basic arithmetic, and plotting on an Argand diagram.
Total Assessment Marks for Algebra: 100

ELABORATION OF CONTENT: FUNCTIONS
WeekTopicElaboration of ContentAssess
7 Functions
  • Function Notation: Understanding \(f(x)\), mapping notation \(f: x \mapsto f(x)\), and interval notation.
  • Domain & Range: Determining domain and range algebraically and graphically. Identifying restrictions on rational (denominator \(\neq 0\)) and radical (value under root \(\ge 0\)) functions.
8 Functions
  • Composite Functions: Evaluating \(f(g(x))\) and \(g(f(x))\), and determining their domains and ranges.
  • Inverse Functions: Defining one-to-one functions and finding the algebraic inverse \(f^{-1}(x)\). Graphing \(f(x)\) and \(f^{-1}(x)\) to show symmetry across \(y=x\). Restricting domains of parabolas for valid inverses.
9 Functions
  • The 6 Parent Functions: In-depth study of Linear, Quadratic, Cubic, Hyperbolic, Exponential, and Logarithmic functions. Analyzing base graphs to understand inherent shapes, symmetry, and asymptotes.
10 Functions
  • Transformations: Applying the general formula \(y = a \cdot f(x-p) + q\) to all parent functions.
  • Shifts & Stretches: Sketching functions with vertical/horizontal shifts, reflections across axes, and stretches.
  • Calculating new intercepts and asymptotes for shifted functions.
11 Functions
  • Inverse Relationship: Deep dive into the relationship between \(y=a^x\) and \(y=\log_a x\).
  • Log Laws: Applying laws of logarithms to simplify expressions and solve exponential/logarithmic equations involving shifts.

ELABORATION OF CONTENT: DIFFERENTIAL CALCULUS
WeekTopicElaboration of ContentAssess
14 Differential Calculus
  • Limits: Informal and formal concept of a limit. Understanding one-sided limits.
  • First Principles: \(f'(x) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h}\).
  • Applying first principles to linear, quadratic, and cubic functions.
15 Differential Calculus
  • Basic Rules: Power rule for polynomials, fractional exponents, and surds. Constant rule and sum/difference rules.
  • Finding the derivative of simple trigonometric functions.
16 Differential Calculus
  • Advanced Rules: Product rule \(\frac{d}{dx}[uv] = u'v + uv'\).
  • Quotient rule \(\frac{d}{dx}\left[\frac{u}{v}\right] = \frac{u'v - uv'}{v^2}\).
  • Chain rule \(\frac{d}{dx}[f(g(x))] = f'(g(x)) \cdot g'(x)\).
17 Differential Calculus
  • Implicit Differentiation: Differentiating equations not in \(y=...\) form (e.g., \(x^2 + y^2 = 25\)).
  • Higher-Order Derivatives: Finding \(f''(x)\) and using it to determine the concavity of a curve.
18 Differential Calculus
  • Tangents & Normals: Calculating equations of tangents and normals to a curve at a specific point.
  • Stationary Points: Finding points where \(f'(x)=0\) and classifying them (max, min, inflection) using \(f''(x)\).
19 Differential Calculus
  • Curve Sketching: Combining intercepts, asymptotes, stationary points, and points of inflection for cubic and quartic functions.
20 Differential Calculus
  • Optimization: Solving real-world problems (maximizing volume, minimizing cost).
  • Related Rates: Solving problems involving changing quantities (e.g., expanding balloons).
 EXAM

ELABORATION OF CONTENT: INTEGRAL CALCULUS
WeekTopicElaboration of ContentAssess
21 Integral Calculus
  • Anti-Differentiation: Introducing the indefinite integral.
  • Power Rule: \(\int x^n dx = \frac{x^{n+1}}{n+1} + C\).
  • Integrating polynomials, fractional powers, and basic trig/exponential functions.
22 Integral Calculus
  • Definite Integrals: Understanding and applying the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus.
  • Evaluating definite integrals \(\int_{a}^{b} f(x) dx\) and understanding their properties.
23 Integral Calculus
  • Techniques: Integration by substitution (u-substitution).
  • Integration by parts: \(\int u \, dv = uv - \int v \, du\).
  • Integrating expressions using partial fractions.
24 Integral Calculus
  • Area: Calculating the area under a curve between two \(x\)-values.
  • Calculating the area bounded between two curves \(f(x)\) and \(g(x)\).
  • Calculating the area bounded by the \(y\)-axis.
25 Integral Calculus
  • Volumes: Calculating volumes of solids of revolution around the x-axis: \(V = \pi \int_{a}^{b} [f(x)]^2 dx\).
  • Calculating volumes of solids of revolution around the y-axis.
26 Integral Calculus
  • Differential Equations: Solving first-order separable equations \(\frac{dy}{dx} = f(x)g(y)\).
  • Modeling exponential growth and decay \(\frac{dy}{dt} = ky\).

ASSESSMENT GUIDELINES (MID-YEAR)

May/June 2027: IFB AQ-L s

This assessment tests major topics taught (approximately 50% of the curriculum complete). It serves as the mid-year benchmark to evaluate student progress before entering the second half of the curriculum.


  • Paper 1: Advanced Algebra, Quartics & Functions. Focuses heavily on algebraic manipulation, complex numbers, and deep function analysis (including all transformations and inverses).
  • Paper 2: Differential Calculus & Integral Calculus. The mark distribution is strictly split: Differential Calculus (35 Marks ± 5) and Integral Calculus (35 Marks ± 5). This ensures a balanced assessment of both halves of the calculus pillar.
  • Paper 3: Trigonometry, Geometry & Probability. Covers advanced trigonometric identities, Euclidean geometry proof structures, and complex probability scenarios (Bayes' Theorem, Venn diagrams).
  • Paper 4: Synthesis & Real-World Applications. A comprehensive paper that merges concepts from multiple pillars, requiring students to apply calculus to physics problems, finance to algebra, and geometry to vectors.

August / September 2027: IFB / AQ and AB Levels

This exam is set uniquely and superficially per student, based on their weak concepts from the mid-year assessments. It is designed to diagnose and remediate specific individual gaps before the final exams.


CRITICAL WARNING:

Students failing this specific assessment are not eligible to write the November/December Final Examinations. This is a strict gatekeeping mechanism to ensure only students who have mastered the required concepts progress to the final exit exam.

ASSESSMENT GUIDELINES (FINAL)

November / December 2027: IFB / A

The final, comprehensive exit examination for the IFB-S Mathematics curriculum. This is the ultimate assessment of a student's mastery over all 9 pillars.


  • Paper 1: Advanced Algebra, Quartics & Functions (150 Marks).
  • Paper 2: Differential Calculus (35±5) & Integral Calculus (35±5) (150 Marks).
  • Paper 3: Trigonometry, Geometry & Probability (150 Marks).
  • Paper 4: Synthesis & Real-World Applications (150 Marks).

Total: 600 Marks


Students must achieve a minimum aggregate of 50% across all four papers to be awarded the IFB-S Mathematics certification.
