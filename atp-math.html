ELABORATION OF CONTENT
Week Topic Elaboration of Content Assess
1 Algebra
  • Mastering the laws of exponents for rational, negative, and zero exponents.
  • Simplifying, multiplying, and rationalising surds (monomial and binomial denominators).
  • Solving exponential equations where bases can be equated, and solving those requiring quadratic substitution (e.g., \(2^{2x} - 5 \cdot 2^x + 4 = 0\)).
2
  • Solving quadratic equations using factorisation, completing the square, and the quadratic formula.
  • Analyzing the nature of roots using the discriminant (\(\Delta = b^2 - 4ac\)) to determine if roots are real, non-real, equal, or distinct.
  • Determining the values of unknown parameters (e.g., \(k\)) to satisfy specific root conditions.
3
  • Applying the Remainder and Factor Theorems to polynomials of degree 3 and 4.
  • Factorising cubic and quartic expressions of the form \(ax^3+bx^2+cx+d\) and \(ax^4+\dots\)
  • Using synthetic division to efficiently find the roots and reduce polynomial degrees.
4
  • Solving simultaneous equations involving one linear and one quadratic equation, both algebraically and graphically.
  • Solving systems of three linear equations with three unknowns using substitution, elimination, or matrices.
  • Translating complex word problems into simultaneous equations and solving them.
5
  • Solving quadratic inequalities using the parabolic method and critical values.
  • Solving rational inequalities (e.g., \(\frac{x-1}{x+3} \ge \frac{x}{x-2}\)) using sign tables and interval notation.
  • Solving absolute value equations and inequalities (linear and quadratic).
6
  • Performing matrix operations: addition, subtraction, scalar multiplication, and matrix multiplication.
  • Calculating determinants and finding the inverse of 2x2 and 3x3 matrices.
  • Solving 2x2 and 3x3 systems using Cramer's Rule and inverse matrices.
  • Introduction to complex numbers: defining \(i^2 = -1\), performing basic arithmetic, and plotting on an Argand diagram.
Total Assessment Marks for Algebra: 100
7 Functions
  • Understanding and using function notation \(f(x)\) and mapping notation \(f: x \mapsto f(x)\).
  • Determining the domain and range of relations algebraically and graphically.
  • Identifying restrictions on rational (denominator \(\neq 0\)) and radical (value under root \(\ge 0\)) functions.
8
  • Evaluating composite functions \(f(g(x))\) and \(g(f(x))\), and determining their domains and ranges.
  • Defining one-to-one functions and finding the algebraic inverse \(f^{-1}(x)\).
  • Graphing \(f(x)\) and \(f^{-1}(x)\) on the same set of axes to show symmetry across the line \(y=x\).
  • Restricting the domain of parabolic functions to ensure their inverse is also a function.
9
  • In-depth study of the 6 parent functions: Linear, Quadratic, Cubic, Hyperbolic, Exponential, and Logarithmic.
  • Analyzing the base graphs without transformations to understand their inherent shape, symmetry, and asymptotes.
10
  • Applying the general transformation formula \(y = a \cdot f(x-p) + q\) to all 6 parent functions.
  • Sketching functions with vertical and horizontal shifts, reflections (across axes), and stretches (vertical and horizontal).
  • Calculating the new intercepts with the axes and the new equations of asymptotes for shifted functions.
11
  • Deep dive into the inverse relationship between \(y=a^x\) and \(y=\log_a x\).
  • Applying the laws of logarithms to simplify expressions and solve exponential/logarithmic equations involving shifts.
Total Assessment Marks for Functions: 100
12 Sequences & Series
  • Analyzing arithmetic sequences: finding the general term and calculating the sum of finite arithmetic series.
  • Analyzing geometric sequences: finding the general term, calculating finite sums, and calculating the sum to infinity for convergent series.
13
  • Quadratic number patterns: finding the general term using the second difference.
  • Using sigma notation to represent and evaluate series.
 TEST
Total Assessment Marks for Sequences & Series: 50
14 Differential Calculus
  • Informal and formal concept of a limit. Understanding one-sided limits.
  • First principles: \(f'(x) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h}\).
  • Applying first principles to linear, quadratic, and cubic functions.
15
  • Power rule for polynomials, fractional exponents, and surds.
  • Constant rule and sum/difference rules.
  • Finding the derivative of simple trigonometric functions.
16
  • Product rule: \(\frac{d}{dx}[uv] = u'v + uv'\).
  • Quotient rule: \(\frac{d}{dx}\left[\frac{u}{v}\right] = \frac{u'v - uv'}{v^2}\).
  • Chain rule: \(\frac{d}{dx}[f(g(x))] = f'(g(x)) \cdot g'(x)\).
17
  • Implicit differentiation for equations not in \(y=...\) form (e.g., \(x^2 + y^2 = 25\)).
  • Finding the second derivative \(f''(x)\) and using it to determine the concavity of a curve.
18
  • Calculating the equations of tangents and normals to a curve at a specific point.
  • Finding stationary points (where \(f'(x)=0\)) and classifying them using the second derivative (local maximum, local minimum, or point of inflection).
19
  • Full curve sketching: combining intercepts, asymptotes, stationary points, and points of inflection for cubic and quartic functions.
20
  • Solving real-world optimization problems (maximizing volume, minimizing surface area/cost).
  • Solving related rates problems (e.g., an expanding balloon, a sliding ladder).
 EXAM
Total Assessment Marks for Differential Calculus: 150
21 Integral Calculus
  • Introducing the concept of an indefinite integral (anti-differentiation).
  • Power rule for integration: \(\int x^n dx = \frac{x^{n+1}}{n+1} + C\).
  • Integrating polynomials, fractional powers, and basic trigonometric/exponential functions.
22
  • Understanding and applying the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus.
  • Evaluating definite integrals \(\int_{a}^{b} f(x) dx\) and understanding the properties of definite integrals.
23
  • Integration by substitution (u-substitution).
  • Integration by parts: \(\int u \, dv = uv - \int v \, du\).
  • Integrating expressions using partial fractions.
24
  • Calculating the area under a curve between two \(x\)-values.
  • Calculating the area bounded between two curves \(f(x)\) and \(g(x)\).
  • Calculating the area bounded by the \(y\)-axis.
25
  • Calculating volumes of solids of revolution around the x-axis: \(V = \pi \int_{a}^{b} [f(x)]^2 dx\).
  • Calculating volumes of solids of revolution around the y-axis.
26
  • Solving first-order separable differential equations: \(\frac{dy}{dx} = f(x)g(y)\).
  • Modeling exponential growth and decay using differential equations: \(\frac{dy}{dt} = ky\).
 TEST
Total Assessment Marks for Integral Calculus: 150
27 Trigonometry
  • Proving complex trigonometric identities using compound angle, double angle, and half-angle formulas.
  • Solving general solutions for trigonometric equations (e.g., \(\sin 2x = \cos x\)).
28
  • Sketching trigonometric graphs with full transformations (amplitude, period, phase shifts).
  • Applying the sine rule, cosine rule, and area rule in 2D and 3D scenarios.
  • Solving problems involving bearings and angles of elevation/depression.
 TEST
Total Assessment Marks for Trigonometry: 100
29 Euclidean Geometry
  • Proving and applying circle theorems: angle at centre = 2x angle at circumference, angle in semi-circle = 90 degrees.
  • Proving cyclic quadrilaterals (opposite angles supplementary, exterior angle = interior opposite angle).
30
  • Proving tangents are perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact.
  • Applying the tan-chord theorem and alternate segment theorem.
  • Proving triangles are similar (equiangular, or sides proportional).
31
  • Applying the proportionality theorem (line parallel to one side of a triangle divides the other two proportionally).
  • Applying the Midpoint theorem and its converse.
  • Proving the Pythagorean theorem using similar triangles.
Total Assessment Marks for Euclidean Geometry: 100
32 Analytical Geometry
  • Using distance, gradient, and midpoint formulas to solve geometric problems.
  • Finding equations of lines (parallel/perpendicular), angle of inclination (\(\tan\theta = m\)).
  • Finding the equation of a circle \((x-a)^2 + (y-b)^2 = r^2\) and the equation of a tangent to a circle.
33
  • Vector notation, addition, subtraction, and scalar multiplication.
  • Calculating the dot product (scalar product) and the angle between two vectors.
  • Calculating the cross product (vector product) and its applications in 3D space.
 TEST
Total Assessment Marks for Analytical Geometry: 100
34 Finance
  • Calculating simple and compound interest, and depreciation (straight-line vs reducing balance).
  • Converting nominal interest rates to effective interest rates.
  • Calculating future value annuities (\(F_v\)) and present value annuities (\(P_v\)).
35
  • Understanding and calculating sinking funds and bonds.
  • Solving for time (\(n\)) and interest rate (\(i\)) using logarithmic equations.
  • Calculating outstanding balances on loans after a specific number of payments.
Total Assessment Marks for Finance: 100
36 Probability
  • Using 2-set and 3-set Venn diagrams to solve probability problems.
  • Drawing tree diagrams for consecutive events (with and without replacement).
  • Distinguishing between mutually exclusive events (\(P(A \text{ and } B) = 0\)) and independent events (\(P(A \text{ and } B) = P(A) \times P(B)\)).
37
  • Using the conditional probability notation \(P(A|B)\).
  • Applying Bayes' Theorem to reverse conditional probabilities.
  • Applying the fundamental counting principle (permutations and combinations) to calculate probabilities.
Total Assessment Marks for Probability: 50
38 Statistics
  • Calculating mean, median, mode, range, and quartiles for grouped and ungrouped data.
  • Drawing and interpreting Ogives (cumulative frequency curves) and box-and-whisker plots.
  • Identifying outliers and analyzing the skewness of a data set.
39
  • Calculating standard deviation and variance to measure the spread of data.
  • Drawing scatter plots and calculating the least squares regression line (\(y = a + bx\)).
  • Interpreting the correlation coefficient (\(r\)) and using the regression line for interpolation/extrapolation.
 MOCK
40
  • Final examination preparation and comprehensive review of all 9 mathematical pillars.
 FINAL
Total Assessment Marks for Statistics: 50
