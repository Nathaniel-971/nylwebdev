COURSE STRUCTURE

The IFB-S Mathematics curriculum is a continuous, 40-week university-level bridge. It operates at maximum capacity, eliminating rote learning to enforce rigorous abstract logic, proof, and advanced computation. This is not a standard high school syllabus; it requires a minimum of 1 to 2 hours of intense, independent study per day. No formula sheets are provided during any assessment; all identities must be derived or memorized.

Assessment Task Structure
Assessment Task Core Mathematical Pillars
Task 1: Algebra & Functions
(Weeks 1-8)		 Algebraic manipulation, Function notation, Composite & Inverse functions, and the 6 Parent Functions (including all vertical/horizontal shifts).
Task 2: Pre-Calculus
(Weeks 9-16)		 Inequalities, Sequences & Series, Limits, Continuity, and the formal definition of the derivative from first principles.
Task 3: Differential Calculus
(Weeks 17-24)		 Advanced differentiation rules, Implicit differentiation, Rates of Change, Optimization, and full Curve Sketching.
Task 4: Integral Calculus
(Weeks 25-32)		 Techniques of integration, Areas and Volumes of revolution, and First-Order Differential Equations.
Task 5: Trig & Geometry
(Weeks 33-40)		 Advanced Trigonometry, 3D Analytical Geometry, Vectors, Probability, Finance, and Final Examination preparation.

ELABORATION OF CONTENT (WEEKS 1-16)
Week Topic Elaboration of Content Assess
1 Algebraic Manipulation
  • Factorising cubic and quartic polynomials using the Factor Theorem.
  • Completing the square to find turning points and solve equations.
  • Partial fractions decomposition (linear, repeated, and irreducible quadratic denominators).
2 Function Notation & Domain
  • Understanding \(f(x)\), \(f: x \mapsto f(x)\), and interval notation.
  • Finding domain and range algebraically and graphically.
  • Identifying restrictions on rational and radical functions.
3 Composite Functions
  • Evaluating \(f(g(x))\) and \(g(f(x))\).
  • Determining the domain and range of composite functions.
  • Decomposing a function into its composite parts.
4 Inverse Functions
  • Understanding the concept of a one-to-one function.
  • Finding the inverse \(f^{-1}(x)\) algebraically.
  • Graphing \(f(x)\) and \(f^{-1}(x)\) (symmetry about \(y=x\)).
  • Restricting the domain to make an inverse a function.
5 The 6 Parent Functions
  • Linear: \(y = mx + c\)
  • Quadratic: \(y = a(x-p)^2 + q\)
  • Cubic: \(y = a(x-p)^3 + q\)
  • Hyperbolic: \(y = \frac{a}{x-p} + q\)
  • Exponential: \(y = a \cdot b^{(x-p)} + q\)
  • Logarithmic: \(y = a \log_b(x-p) + q\)
6 Transformations & Shifts
  • Applying the transformation \(a \cdot f(x-p) + q\) to all 6 parent functions.
  • Sketching functions with shifts, reflections, and stretches.
  • Finding asymptotes and intercepts of transformed functions.
7 Exponential & Log Graphs
  • Deep dive into the inverse relationship of \(y=a^x\) and \(y=\log_a x\).
  • Solving exponential and log equations with shifts.
8 Task 1 Assessment
  • Synthesis of algebra, composites, inverses, and 6 shifted functions.
 TEST
9 Inequalities
  • Solving quadratic, polynomial, and rational inequalities.
  • Using critical values and sign tables.
  • Absolute value equations and inequalities.
10 Sequences & Series
  • Arithmetic and Geometric sequences.
  • Sum of finite and infinite series.
  • Sigma notation and limits of series.
11 Limits (Part 1)
  • Informal and formal concept of a limit.
  • Evaluating limits numerically, graphically, and algebraically.
  • One-sided limits and limits at infinity.
12 Limits (Part 2) & Continuity
  • Using L'Hôpital's Rule for indeterminate forms (\(0/0\), \(\infty/\infty\)).
  • Testing for continuity at a point and over an interval.
  • Identifying removable and non-removable discontinuities.
13 The Derivative as a Limit
  • Average rate of change vs. instantaneous rate of change.
  • First principles: \(f'(x) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h}\).
  • Applying first principles to quadratics and cubics.
14 Differentiability
  • Relationship between continuity and differentiability.
  • Identifying points where a function is not differentiable.
15 Task 2 Assessment
  • Synthesis of inequalities, series, limits, and first principles.
 TEST
16 Revision
  • Review of Task 1 & 2 high-risk topics.

Week Topic Elaboration of Content Assess
17 Differentiation Rules (1)
  • Power rule for polynomials, fractional exponents, and surds.
  • Constant rule and sum/difference rules.
18 Differentiation Rules (2)
  • Product rule: \(\frac{d}{dx}[uv] = u'v + uv'\).
  • Quotient rule: \(\frac{d}{dx}\left[\frac{u}{v}\right] = \frac{u'v - uv'}{v^2}\).
  • Chain rule: \(\frac{d}{dx}[f(g(x))] = f'(g(x)) \cdot g'(x)\).
19 Implicit & Higher-Order
  • Implicit differentiation for equations not in \(y=...\) form.
  • Finding the second derivative \(f''(x)\).
  • Using the second derivative to determine concavity.
20 Applications (1)
  • Equations of tangents and normals.
  • Finding stationary points (where \(f'(x)=0\)).
  • Classifying stationary points using \(f''(x)\) (max, min, inflection).
21 Applications (2)
  • Curve sketching: combining intercepts, asymptotes, and turning points.
  • Sketching cubic and quartic functions.
22 Optimization
  • Real-world optimization problems (maximizing volume, minimizing surface area/cost).
  • Setting up constraint equations and objective functions.
23 Related Rates
  • Solving related rates problems (e.g., expanding balloons, sliding ladders).
  • Using the chain rule to relate different rates of change.
24 Task 3 Assessment
  • Synthesis of differentiation rules and applications.
 EXAM
25 Anti-Differentiation
  • The concept of an indefinite integral.
  • Power rule for integration: \(\int x^n dx = \frac{x^{n+1}}{n+1} + C\).
  • Integrating polynomials, fractional powers, and basic trig/exponential functions.
26 Definite Integrals
  • The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus.
  • Evaluating definite integrals \(\int_{a}^{b} f(x) dx\).
27 Integration Techniques
  • Integration by substitution (u-substitution).
  • Integration by parts: \(\int u \, dv = uv - \int v \, du\).
  • Integrating partial fractions.
28 Applications: Area
  • Calculating the area under a curve between two \(x\)-values.
  • Calculating the area between two curves \(f(x)\) and \(g(x)\).
29 Applications: Volume
  • Volumes of solids of revolution around the x-axis: \(V = \pi \int_{a}^{b} [f(x)]^2 dx\).
30 Differential Equations
  • Solving first-order separable differential equations: \(\frac{dy}{dx} = f(x)g(y)\).
  • Exponential growth and decay models: \(\frac{dy}{dt} = ky\).
31 Task 4 Assessment
  • Synthesis of integration and differential equations.
 TEST

34 Trig Graphs & Applications
  • Sketching trigonometric graphs with transformations.
  • Sine rule, cosine rule, and area rule in 2D and 3D.
35 3D Geometry & Vectors

  • Vectors in 2D and 3D space.
  • Dot product and cross product.
36 Euclidean Geometry

37 Probability & Statistics

  • Standard deviation, variance, and regression analysis.
38 Financial Mathematics

EXAMINATION GUIDELINES

This section clearly separates the formal testing schedule from the teaching plan. The IFB-S Mathematics examination structure for the 2027 academic year is strictly divided into three distinct phases.

1. May/June 2027: IFB AQ-L s

This assessment tests major topics taught (approximately 50% of the curriculum complete). It serves as the mid-year benchmark.

  • Paper 1: Advanced Algebra, Quartics & Functions.
  • Paper 2: Differential Calculus (35 Marks ± 5) & Integral Calculus (35 Marks ± 5).
  • Paper 3: Trigonometry, Geometry & Probability.
  • Paper 4: Synthesis & Real-World Applications.
2. August / September 2027: IFB / AQ and AB Levels

This exam is set uniquely and superficially per student, based on their weak concepts from the mid-year assessments. It is designed to diagnose and remediate specific individual gaps before the final exams.

Warning: Students failing this specific assessment are not eligible to write the November/December Final Examinations.

3. November / December 2027: IFB / A

The final, comprehensive exit examination for the IFB-S Mathematics curriculum.

  • Paper 1: Advanced Algebra, Quartics & Functions (150 Marks).
  • Paper 2: Differential Calculus & Integral Calculus (150 Marks).
  • Paper 3: Trigonometry, Geometry & Probability (150 Marks).
  • Paper 4: Synthesis & Real-World Applications (150 Marks).
