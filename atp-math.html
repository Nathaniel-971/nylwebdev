CURRICULUM OVERVIEW

The IFB AE-S Mathematics curriculum is designed as a rigorous, university-level bridging course. It does not conform to standard national curricula; rather, it is structured to eliminate rote learning and force students to master abstract, logical, and computational thinking.


The curriculum is strictly divided into three pillars:

  • Pure Algebra: Focuses on complex simultaneous equations, advanced manipulation of surds, exponents, and strict inequalities.
  • Functions: Involves deep analysis of parabolic, cubic, exponential, and logarithmic graphs, including transformation and inverse functions.
  • Calculus: Covers first principles, advanced differentiation, optimization, and rates of change without the use of a formula sheet.

Assessment Structure: The course features four rigorous examination papers (Paper 1, 2, 3, and 4) totaling 600 Marks. No formula sheets are provided; students must derive or memorize all necessary mathematical identities.

TERM 1: ALGEBRA & FUNCTIONS
Week Topic Content & Sub-topics
1 - 2 Advanced Exponents & Surds Solving equations with fractional exponents. Simplifying nested radicals. Rationalizing complex denominators.
3 - 4 Complex Algebra Simultaneous equations involving quadratics and cubics. Rational inequalities (e.g., \(\frac{x-1}{x+3} \geq \frac{x}{x-2}\)).
5 - 6 Nature of Roots Using the discriminant (\(\Delta\)) for quadratics and cubic derivatives. Determining values of \(k\) for equal, real, or non-real roots.
7 - 8 Parabolic & Cubic Functions Finding turning points, points of inflection, intercepts, and sketching without a calculator. Maximum distance between two graphs.
9 - 10 Exponential & Log Functions Graphing \(y=a^x\) and \(y=\log_a x\). Determining inverses. Transformations and asymptotes. Logarithms without a calculator.

TERM 2: DIFFERENTIAL CALCULUS
Week Topic Content & Sub-topics
1 - 2 First Principles Deriving the derivative using \(f'(x) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h}\). Application to quadratics and cubics.
3 - 4 Derivative Rules Power rule for polynomials, fractional exponents, and surds. Product rule and basic chain rule.
5 - 6 Tangents & Normals Finding the equation of the tangent line (\(y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)\)) and the normal line to a curve at a given point.
7 - 8 Cubic Graphs & Calculus Using \(f'(x)=0\) to find stationary points. Using \(f''(x)\) to determine the nature of stationary points (max, min, inflection).
9 - 10 Optimization & Rates Real-world application problems: maximizing volume of boxes, minimizing surface area, and calculating velocity/acceleration.
11 Term 2 Assessment AE-S Paper 2: Analytical Geometry & Calculus (150 Marks).

TERM 3: SERIES, FINANCE & TRIG
Week Topic Content & Sub-topics
1 - 2 Sequences & Series Arithmetic and Geometric sequences. Sum to infinity. Sigma notation. Quadratic number patterns and second differences.
3 - 4 Financial Mathematics Compound interest, depreciation (straight-line and reducing balance), nominal vs effective interest rates, and annuities.
5 - 6 Trigonometry Basics Reduction formulas, special angles, and solving basic trigonometric equations without a calculator.
7 - 8 Trig Identities & Equations Proving complex identities. General solutions for sine, cosine, and tangent. Compound and double angle formulas.
9 - 10 2D & 3D Trigonometry Sine rule, Cosine rule, and Area rule. Solving non-right-angled triangles in real-world 3D scenarios.
11 Term 3 Assessment AE-S Paper 3: Sequences, Finance & Trigonometry (150 Marks).
